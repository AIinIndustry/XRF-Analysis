\section{Denoising}

\begin{frame}{XRF Denoising}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{The Problem:} XRF spectra are often corrupted by noise and background artifacts.
        \item \textbf{The Impact:} Noise can obscure low-intensity peaks, leading to incorrect elemental identification or inaccurate quantification.
        \item \textbf{The Objective:} "Chemical signal recovery": mapping a noisy raw input spectrum to its clean, noise-free ground-truth counterpart.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Dataset Generation}
    To synthesize the required (X, Y) training pairs, we developed a high-performance generator:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Decisional Logic (\texttt{n\_counts}):} Every pair $(X, Y)$ is generated by fixing a specific physical state $\theta$ and synthesizing two versions via the \textbf{\texttt{multiel\_spectra}} engine:
        \begin{itemize}
            \item \textbf{Ground Truth ($Y$):} \texttt{n\_counts = 1} (minimal noise, simulator floor).
            \item \textbf{Noisy Observation ($X$):} \texttt{n\_counts} $\in [10000, 30000]$ (stochastic degradation).
        \end{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Generic Learning:} This variety prevents overfitting to a fixed noise profile, forcing the model to learn pure signal recovery.
        \item \textbf{Dataset Size:} 2000 paired samples; split 70/15/15 (train/val/test).
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Preprocessing Strategies}
    XRF spectra present a unique \textbf{"Peak vs. Baseline"} challenge: peaks can be orders of magnitude larger than the background.
    
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Standard Scaling:} Normalizes by mean and standard deviation; preserves the natural peak-to-background ratio and proved most robust across all architectures.
        \item \textbf{MinMax Scaling:} Normalizes to [0,1]; often suppresses trace signals in the presence of dominant peaks.
        \item \textbf{LogMinMaxScaler:} Applies $\log(1+x)$ followed by MinMax scaling; theoretically compresses dynamic range to protect low-intensity peaks, but did not consistently outperform Standard scaling in practice.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Model Architectures (1/2)}
    We evaluated 8 diverse architectures to find the best structural fit for 1D spectral data:
    \vspace{0.2cm}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{CNN (1D):} Standard convolutional autoencoder. Uses local kernels to detect and "smooth" peak shapes.
        \item \textbf{UNet1D:} Deep encoder-decoder with skip-connections. Excellent for preserving high-resolution peak positions while suppressing noise.
        \item \textbf{Transformer:} Treats spectral channels as a token sequence. Applies self-attention across the full spectrum to model relationships between distant energy regions.
        \item \textbf{Conformer:} Combines local convolutions (shape detection) with global attention (peak relationships).
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Model Architectures (2/2)}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{ResNet1D:} Utilizes residual blocks to allow deeper feature extraction without losing original signal information.
        \item \textbf{LSTM:} Recurrent autoencoder. Processes the spectrum as a sequence of energy steps.
        \item \textbf{MLP:} Fully connected autoencoder.
        \item \textbf{Linear:} Linear bottleneck.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Training Objective Design}
    We systematically evaluated \textbf{4 loss strategies} defined by two orthogonal choices:
    \vspace{0.2cm}
    \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{lcc}
        \toprule
        & \textbf{No Sparsity} & \textbf{+ L1 on Background} \\
        \midrule
        \textbf{Global MSE} & Standard MSE & Standard MSE + L1 \\
        \textbf{Masked MSE} & Masked MSE & Masked MSE + L1 \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    \vspace{0.2cm}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Global vs.\ Masked:} Whether the MSE loss is computed over the full spectrum or only over peak regions.
        \item \textbf{L1 Sparsity:} Penalizes any non-zero prediction in flat background areas, actively suppressing residual artifacts that MSE alone tends to leave behind.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

                \item \textbf{Standard Scaler:} Preserves the peak-to-background ratio, ideal for the Conformer's dual inductive biases.
                \item \textbf{No Masking:} Global MSE suffices; attention naturally focuses on informative regions.
